Part 3. 
WOMAN: Good morning. And in the studio today we have Lucas Doran, who is in charge of the 
monkey house at the zoo, where not only monkeys, but also the big apes such as gorillas are kept. 
Lucas, welcome. How did you get to work with gorillas? 
LUCAS: I worked at the zoo for some time. I began with the snakes, which was brilliant, and then 
moved on to the rhinos. My ambition was always to work with big cats like lions and tigers. So when 
they transferred me to the monkey house, I was disappointed at first. But later on I realised how lucky 
I was because monkeys are so clever. 
WOMAN: That must keep you on your toes. Tell us about your day. 
LUCAS: I get to work about 7:45, and the first job is to look at the animals. Nobody’s on duty at 
night, so we have to make sure none of them is ill, or whether any babies have been born. You see, 
most monkeys give birth at night. Then we clean the cages and change the water. Then later on in the 
day we put down fresh straw for their bedding. 
WOMAN: What about feeding the animals?

--- PAGE 2 ---
LUCAS: They’re fed four times a day in summer and three in winter. The monkeys eat anything, 
really. Fruit, vegetables, cooked meat, insects, but grapes are their favourite, though. 
WOMAN: Have you ever been hurt by one of the animals? 
LUCAS: Once. A young female gorilla got out one day. I was just sweeping a path and she put a 
hand on my chest and pushed me out of the way, enough to knock me off my feet. I fell over and 
broke my arm. Fortunately, just then her baby cried and she ran back inside. 
WOMAN: Oh gosh. Now, I’d like to move on to your relationship with the public. 
LUCAS: Well, a big part of my job is helping people to understand about the animals. Lots of families 
come at the weekend and I answer their questions. And I especially enjoy my talks with the students 
who come during the week. 
LUCAS: But what I really can’t stand is when people feed the monkeys. It’s not so bad if they give 
them fruit. But people should know that things like cake are not good for them. 
WOMAN: Do you have any funny stories? 
LUCAS: One time I had to look after two newborn baby monkeys. I had to take them back to my flat 
in a box on the bus because my car had broken down. They slept most of the way, but I got some very 
strange looks when these hairy little fingers crept out of the box. 
WOMAN: And what of the future? 
LUCAS: I’ve recently gone back to studying because I’m interested in running my own zoo one day. 
At first I thought I needed zoology, but actually the most useful course turned out to be one in biology. 
I was always good at maths and sciences, so I’m really enjoying it. 
LUCAS: But what I’d really like to do is visit Africa, not as a tourist, but to meet the famous 
conservationist Bridget Foley and see the park where she does her research into apes and monkeys. 
It would be really exciting to see the animals in their natural surroundings. 
WOMAN: Well, Lucas, best of luck, and thank you for joining us today. 
LUCAS: Thank you.
