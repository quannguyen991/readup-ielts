Narrator: Generative AI is everywhere. Even if you haven’t used it yourself, you might have heard about it or come across social media posts using it - pictures of how friends might look in 20 years’ time, or as an action figure doll.
So, what is Gen AI? In a nutshell, generative artificial intelligence is a computer technology capable of creating text, images, audio, and videos incredibly quickly. And it’s not just used for fun. Every day, hundreds of millions of people use Gen AI to write emails, develop ideas, and even solve scientific challenges.
You access generative AI through a chatbot. It’s like having a conversation. Every time you ask an AI chatbot a question or prompt it to perform a task, it predicts what you would like as a response, based on analysing millions and millions of pieces of data that it’s already catalogued. You can even get it to answer with a particular tone - for example, chatty, funny, or deadly serious; or, if you’re using audio, a particular voice. It’s getting so good at this, it can almost feel like chatting to a person.
Chatbots like Copilot or ChatGPT can be used as assistants that can help with a range of tasks: everything from prioritising your to-do list, to planning your next holiday, or drafting a best man’s speech.
So, how do you get started with an AI chatbot? Getting set up with an account is pretty straightforward. If your school or employer already has guidelines, follow their instructions. Once you’re set up, start asking it questions and see how it responds.
It’s easy to be impressed when it rewrites your CV or plans a round-the-world trip for you in seconds. But before you start relying on it, you need to understand a few key things:
Firstly, the responses you get can look and sound very convincing, but that doesn’t mean they’re right, or the information in them is true. Known as AI hallucinations, there have been cases where chatbots have advised users to add glue to pizza, eat rocks, or given dubious medical or legal information. You really do need to check the information they give you against reliable sources. And that doesn’t just mean a quick cross-check with Google; most search engines now provide summaries to queries which are themselves generated by AI, so they can also be misleading. So don’t just copy and paste what the chatbot spits out; you could be sharing incorrect or even dangerous information. And you’ll notice the phrasing can come across as inauthentic and not like you.
Secondly, every time you enter a question or share information with a chatbot, you’re helping to train it - making it more intelligent, if you like. So a simple rule is to always ask yourself: Would I be happy sharing this information with someone on a bus? Am I okay with Google, Microsoft, or another technology company storing this information about me forever and potentially using it to train their AI systems? Be really careful before entering private or sensitive personal information. If you’re using it for work, always follow your employer’s data privacy guidelines.
Thirdly, Gen AI uses up a huge amount of energy. Providing an AI response needs about 10 times more electricity than a Google search, and vast amounts of water. Its capabilities aren’t necessary for really basic questions, like “What’s the weather in Sydney today?”
Those are the watch-outs. But when you do use it, how can you get the most out of it? Asking questions - also known as prompts - in the right way really matters. Be specific, provide context, and be prepared to start again and rephrase if you want a better response. “Suggest a packing list for a weekend in Paris in June, which includes formal and casual clothing appropriate for a 30-year-old British woman” should get you a much more useful answer than “What do I need for visiting Paris?”
And think about tone. If you want help with an email, give them information about who you’re writing to and how you want to come across: “Help me draft an email to the council asking for a parking ticket refund using a polite but persuasive tone” should give you a better draft than “Make the council give me my money back.” And, of course, check the draft is correct and expressed in the way you want it to be.
Remember, generative AI has created a set of handy tools, but they aren’t perfect, and you definitely need to cross-check everything they say.
