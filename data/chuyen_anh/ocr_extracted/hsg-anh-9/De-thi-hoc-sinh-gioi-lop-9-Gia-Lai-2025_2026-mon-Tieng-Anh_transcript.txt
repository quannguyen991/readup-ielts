Oh, and if you want more information afterwards, I have copies of a fact sheet from the Royal Life 
Saving Society here, and you can also visit our website. The address is printed on the back. 
To most parents, teaching very small kids to swim isn’t a high priority. You can take your baby to 
the pool when it’s as young as three months. By the age of six months, the child should be able to 
begin having lessons. Afterwards, you should take your child to the swimming pool as often as 
possible. After a few lessons, even very young children can be taught to just roll over and float on 
their backs, blow bubbles, and generally enjoy life. 
Of course, as parents, you can do much more. Make sure someone is always supervising children 
around the pool. As a rule, there should be one supervising adult for every four kids under eight. And 
there’s a simple message to tell your kids: if there’s no adult around, you can’t go near the water. 
Talking of private pools, these should be your biggest worry in the summer months. By law, you must 
have a securely fastened fence around the pool. It’s also a good idea to have a back door alarm that 
makes a loud sound if your child ever decides to explore outside by him or herself. 
When you go to the beach, you have to be extra careful. Always keep an eye on your child. Also, 
avoid all those kind of blow-up toys that kids love. And, this may seem an obvious thing to say, but 
you’d be amazed how many people ignore it: never, I repeat never, swim when the red flag is flying. 
Apart from that, have a great time at the beach!
