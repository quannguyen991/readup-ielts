The problem

A question you can answer without
reading the passage does not measure reading.

It measures background knowledge, or the shape of four options.

Learners drilled on such questions learn to guess — and their measured progress is partly fictional.

No formal rule catches it

"According to the passage,
is water wet?"

Correctly formatted. Quotes the source verbatim. Four well-formed options. And useless.

Every AI tutor now generates items this way. None ship the item's provenance.

The check

Make a second model try to answer it
with the passage taken away.

Model A writes an item
→
7 structural rules
→
Model B answers it blind
→
serve, or discard

If Model B gets it right and reports the answer was determinable, the item is not testing reading. It is discarded, and the reason is fed back so the next draft does not fail the same way.

Model B is never asked whether the question is good. That would be another unverifiable judgement. It is given the one task that can be checked — answer it — and a rule engine with tests draws the conclusion.

AI is the engine and the subject. Remove it and there is nothing left to audit.

Why the question is sharper than it looks

The prompt already forbids this.
We measure what happens anyway.

Rules 8–13, already in the generation prompt

No copying sentences from the passage.
No building FALSE by inserting "not".
No distractors that give themselves away.
No answers that always sit in paragraph one.

So this is not "does AI get this wrong".

It is how often it still does after being told, explicitly, not to.

A high rate after a prohibition says something a high rate with no instruction does not.

Instruction-following is not a safety guarantee. That is the finding's real shape.

Discipline

The threshold was committed to git
before the measurement first ran.

8% — below it, the pre-written conclusion is that the gate catches less than it costs and is not worth running at scale.

200 blind runs minimum — below it the function refuses to conclude anything at all.

Three conclusions written out in full, one per outcome. Choosing between them is arithmetic, not judgement.

src/services/provenance/ledger.ts export const FALSIFICATION_THRESHOLD = 0.08; export const MIN_SAMPLE = 200;

It lives in code, not in a document that can be edited at any time. Git history is the evidence for the order.

A disappointing result cannot be reinterpreted after it is seen.

7 / 14

The field that matters most

not-run is never merged into passed.

When a provider call fails, the item is still served — blocking a learner because our own gate broke punishes the wrong person. But the label records that nothing was checked.

Lying about having checked turns the whole receipt into decoration. A test fails if the two states are ever merged.

We found this the hard way: a type bug in our own reader silently marked 18% of items "not checked" when the judge had in fact answered. The honest-by-default design meant the error understated our own result rather than inflating it — and made it visible.

Published counts always separate rejected from could not be checked.

The verifier

It prints what it cannot establish.
No flag turns those lines off.

✓ signature valid did:key:z6MkvrEz7KFP9QeRhm8xvaqrFdk7Wc9… ✓ 4 skills recorded, 2 confirmed by transfer to a different source This does NOT establish: ⚠ that the holder did not cheat while answering ⚠ that any institution recognises this — it is self-issued ⚠ that the cited item labels exist, until the corpus is published

That output came from a credential issued in the browser and verified by a standalone script — two separate runtimes, one chain. A receipt made only of ticks reads as a certificate, which is the misunderstanding this exists to prevent.

Tamper with one field and the signature fails, loudly.

The human anchor

Without people, this is a machine
adjudicating a machine.

Three to eight learners answer ~100 of the same items with the passages withheld — asked the same two questions the model was asked: what is your answer, and were you sure or guessing.

The worksheet mixes half the items the model rejected with half it passed. Random sampling would measure the false-alarm rate and barely measure the miss rate — and misses are worse, because a miss sends a useless item straight to a learner.

Why eight people is the right number

Eight cannot support a learning-outcomes study. We cut the study of that shape rather than run an underpowered one.

Eight comfortably supports an agreement rate — which is exactly the claim that needs anchoring.

The agreement figure will be published even if it is poor.

Protocol includes the sentences the facilitator must not say.

10 / 14

What we will not claim

The limits, before you ask for them.

  • ✗Not "proven to improve learning." There is no efficacy data from a real cohort. TRL 6, not 7.
  • ✗Not "a recognised credential." It is self-issued. Portable and independently checkable — never "recognised".
  • ✗Not "the gate runs on every question." Today it runs in the measurement harness and on transfer probes. Moving it onto every served item is next.
  • ⚠"Answerable blind" is a lower bound on leakage, not a verdict on pedagogical worth. Real exams contain such items too.
  • ⚠The judge is itself a model, and the corpus is Wikipedia, Gutenberg and PLOS — not real exam passages.

A project whose thesis is honest measurement cannot be selective about its own.

Sustainable Development Goals

Where this actually lands.

  • 4Quality education. Learners stop being drilled on items that do not test what they claim to — and the rate of such items becomes a published number.
  • 10Reduced inequalities. did:key needs no ledger, no server and no connectivity, so a learner with none of those still owns a checkable record of their ability.
  • 9Industry & infrastructure. The gate, the label format and the verifier are documented and reusable — any vendor can run this against their own pipeline.
  • 16Institutions. A claim about a person is auditable by that person, without trusting whoever made it.

The reusable part is the method, not the product.
